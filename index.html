Understanding Focal Length & Crop Factors

Does the focal length of the lens actually change?

No. Focal length is a physical property of a lens (the distance from the optical center of the lens to the sensor when focused at infinity). A 50mm lens is always a 50mm lens, whether it is designed for a Full Frame camera or a crop-sensor (APS-C) camera.

Why does the crop factor exist?

An APS-C sensor is physically smaller than a standard 35mm Full Frame sensor. Because the sensor is smaller, it only captures a smaller rectangle out of the middle of the lens's projected image circle. This crops the image, reducing the field of view. The resulting image looks exactly like a longer focal length shot captured on a Full Frame camera.

The 1.5x Crop Multiplier

For Sony, Nikon, Fujifilm, Pentax, and Sigma crop-sensor cameras, the conversion factor is approximately 1.5x (Canon is 1.6x).

  • Focal Length Multiplying: Mounting a 50mm lens on an APS-C body yields the equivalent field of view of a 75mm lens on a Full Frame body ($50\text{mm} \times 1.5 = 75\text{mm}$).
  • Depth of Field (DoF): While the focal length equivalence applies to the field of view, the depth of field also changes. An f/2.8 lens on APS-C will yield the equivalent depth of field of an f/4.2 lens on Full Frame ($2.8 \times 1.5 = 4.2$) in terms of background blur, though the light gathering capability per unit area remains f/2.8.
